# Gameplay pacing and landscape depth

Local terrain starts at **6x simulation time**. Terrain settings select 1x engineering, 6x exploration or 12x fast travel. TAB switches the chosen accelerated pace and real time. The physical rover speed remains 0.04 m/s; nominal wall-clock travel is 0.24 or 0.48 m/s. Physics ticks scale with time (120/720/1440 Hz) to preserve integration step size. This costs CPU time, particularly at 12x; this is time acceleration rather than an unvalidated faster rover model. Camera smoothing and particle animation retain wall-clock pacing. The original field lab and rover parameter file are unchanged.

The generation worker now prepares a sourced annulus extending 20 km beyond the patch. Its geometry follows the same radial reference and geographic sampling service, including planetary curvature. This is actual 3D scenery with camera parallax, not a painted mountain backdrop. Available source coverage determines relief; flat areas can remain flat. Scenery is coarsely sampled, uses a separate registered imagery atlas, has no collision and does not enlarge the playable region. The generation revision invalidates old geometry/imagery artifacts. Local camera far clipping is 30 km; distant precision and coarse silhouettes are limitations.

The existing atmospheric integration includes subtly moving low dust banks in world coordinates. Dust bank density and height vary along the view ray; near terrain remains clear. These banks are illustrative weather and share the atmosphere strength/quality and Sun-dependent sky radiance. They do not receive volumetric shadow maps or produce shadowed light shafts. Godot Compatibility does not support native volumetric fog: [renderer capabilities](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html).

`procedural_terrain/close_gravel.gd` streams nine deterministic 8 m cells around the camera, preparing at most one cell per rendered frame. Each contains 120 cosmetic pebbles aligned with terrain normals. Instanced pebbles receive the shared Sun/sky lighting and cast shadows. Larger rock normals are recomputed from their irregular geometry while retaining identical collision vertices.

`procedural_terrain/wheel_tracks.gd` now manages a shared rolling height/compaction mask instead of layered ribbons. All six wheels merge into this mask, using integrated travel distance for tread phase. Small dense visual terrain tiles replace ready areas of the original render mesh and use the same imagery and surface material. Shallow illustrative ruts reach 15 mm, with roughly 3 mm shoulders; collision and measured source heights are unchanged. Source appearance restores the original ground. See [Ground impression correction](TRACK_IMPRESSIONS.md) for the current implementation and verification.

Verification evidence: `evidence/gameplay_polish.json`, rendered `dusty_gameplay_tracks_*` images and `dusty_gameplay_source_1600x900.png`. The verification script covers pacing, grounded driving, bounded marks, source switching, sourced scenery, nearby scatter and measured-height invariance, with frame-time samples at the three supported sizes.

The earlier ribbon implementation’s graphical check passed all 14 assertions, including upward-facing tread normals. The combined batch also passed 25 procedural terrain checks, 11 camera checks and 48 globe checks; the original rover suite completed all 24 measurement cases. No production script or shader compilation errors remained. Existing GL texture/ObjectDB shutdown warnings persist. A file-format error on the first launch was repaired; visual review then corrected track triangle winding, with only the affected graphical checks repeated.

GTX 1650, Balanced, 6x exploration: three-second moving-camera samples measured median/p95 **20.82/36.52 ms at 1920x1080**, **14.80/26.94 ms at 1600x900** and **12.33/17.14 ms at 1366x768**. The 12x stationary-camera sample at 1600x900 measured 13.92/21.00 ms. Different camera motion makes these samples unsuitable as a direct 6x-versus-12x CPU comparison. The 1080p maximum frame was 238.03 ms; the largest refinement upload batch was 2.89 ms. Static memory was approximately 434 MiB, with 228–265 MiB of texture memory reported separately. Cold default preparation measured 15.37 s and warm preparation 0.41 s in the procedural regression. These short available-laptop observations do not establish sustained 60 FPS. Refinement stalls, coarse distant textures and shadow cleanup remain limitations.
